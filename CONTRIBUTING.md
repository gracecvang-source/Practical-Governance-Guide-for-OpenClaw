# Contributing

Thank you for helping improve this guide.

This repo is intended to support practical governance thinking for OpenClaw. Contributions should make the guide clearer, more useful, more grounded, and easier for a community or project team to adapt.

## What belongs here

Good contributions may include:

- clearer governance language
- practical decision-making templates
- better meeting or proposal workflows
- role and responsibility examples
- conflict-resolution process examples
- risk and accountability notes
- readability improvements
- scope and limitation clarifications

## Core principles

Please keep contributions aligned with these principles:

- Governance should be practical, not performative.
- Decision-making should be legible to the people affected by it.
- Roles should include responsibilities and limits.
- Processes should distinguish proposals, discussions, decisions, and implementation.
- Accountability should be specific enough to use.
- The guide should avoid pretending to be legal advice or official policy unless adopted by the relevant project.

## Scope boundaries

Do not contribute material that presents this guide as:

- legal advice
- a binding constitution
- official OpenClaw policy unless explicitly adopted
- a replacement for project maintainers or community decision-making
- a universal governance model for every community
- a way to centralize control while calling it governance

## Style guide

Write in plain language.

Prefer:

- short sections
- concrete examples
- reusable templates
- clear definitions
- practical checklists
- honest limitations

Avoid:

- inflammatory language
- vague values statements with no process
- governance jargon without explanation
- overclaiming
- assuming every project has the same power structure

## Pull request checklist

Before submitting a pull request, check:

- [ ] The contribution fits the scope of a practical governance guide.
- [ ] The language does not imply legal authority or official adoption unless true.
- [ ] Proposals, discussions, decisions, and implementation are clearly distinguished.
- [ ] New templates or workflows are reusable.
- [ ] Sensitive or private community information is not included.
- [ ] The tone is constructive and grounded.
- [ ] Any new files are linked from the README if needed.

## Reporting issues

When opening an issue, include:

- what part of the guide is confusing, incomplete, or risky
- what kind of governance situation you were thinking about
- what improvement you suggest, if any

Please do not include private community conflicts, personal accusations, or sensitive internal project details in public issues.
